# UPDATE 07:12Z — RE-ANALYSIS CHANGES THE RECOMMENDATION (author asked why SQ won)

The area result is **mean-latency-only**, is **not** a topoB artefact, and **reverses at the tail**.

## It was already true on the submitted cluster

| λ=12, MAIN topology (v1exact) | mean e2e | p99 e2e |
|---|---|---|
| passthrough **SQ** | **2,050.9** | 10,954.5 |

| passthrough **RR** | 2,669.9 | 11,226.4 |
| RouteBalance uniform | 2,229.5 | **5,389.3** |

RouteBalance beats passthrough **round-robin** — which is exactly the row §5.4 tabulates ("a
passthrough floor … with round-robin dispatch"), so the paper is internally consistent. Against
**shortest-queue** it was already ~8% behind on the mean, on the submitted cluster, at both rates.
topoB did not create this: swapping 3B 3→5 and 7B 5→3 added capacity in precisely the tier a
queue-based dispatcher fills (SQ mix: 3B 55.0%; RB: 14B 56.6%), widening 8% → 26%.

## The tail runs the other way, everywhere

RouteBalance's p99 is **roughly half** every passthrough variant's, on both topologies and both
rates: 5,389 vs ~11,000 (λ12 main) · 5,741 vs 11,120 (λ24 main) · 5,640 vs 10,745 (λ12 topoB) ·
5,980 vs 11,046 (λ24 topoB). That is what routing hard prompts to larger tiers should buy — a
bounded tail for a slightly worse typical case.

Decomposition of RB's λ24 topoB mean deficit (582.6 ms): ~168 ms scheduler-side residual
(207.6 vs 39.6 ms median), remainder is genuinely serving larger models (TPOT 26.1 vs 16.2 ms/tok).
At the same point RB also **wins throughput** (23.24 vs 22.66) and **quality** (0.5057 vs 0.4899).

## Revised recommendation — A′ (stronger than the claim it replaces)

Reframe W1 as: re-provisioning preserves the operating region and weight control; the trained
router **collapses** (24.8×); and RouteBalance **halves the p99** against every dispatcher-only
