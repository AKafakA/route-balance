# NeurIPS 2026 Review — RouteBalance: Fused Model Routing and Load Balancing for Heterogeneous LLM Serving

**Severity**: Standard.
**Reviewer rubric**: weak-accept-bar, actively scouting rejection-grade issues.

## Summary

The paper proposes RouteBalance, a serving-aware control layer that fuses model routing (which model size to use) and instance-level load balancing (which replica) into a single online assignment over concrete model instances of a heterogeneous cluster. The decision is a per-batch argmax over a three-term scoring function combining a raw KNN-predicted quality score and batch-max-normalised cost and end-to-end latency, weighted on a 3-simplex. The deployed predictor stack is one batched MiniLM embedding + FAISS KNN over a 14,919-prompt labelled set for quality and expected output length, plus a per-(model, GPU) XGBoost head for TPOT (E2E latency derived analytically from predicted TPOT × queue state). A dead-reckoning instance-state cache lets within-batch dispatches reflect the post-assignment view. Evaluation is on a 13-instance CloudLab cluster (2× A100 / 3× V100 / 5× A30 / 3× A30) hosting four Qwen2.5 sizes (3B–72B), comparing RouteBalance against AvengersPro, BEST-Route, and a passthrough router with three dispatchers, with all baselines run inside RouteBalance's own scheduler/batching path ("pipeline mode") for architecture-controlled fairness. The headline claim is an upper-bound region of the three-way quality–latency–cost Pareto frontier together with a sub-linear scheduling-overhead curve (149 ms at λ=12, 220 ms at λ=30).

## Strengths

1. **Clean problem framing.** The router-vs-balancer split in current systems is real and the "one decision over instances" reformulation is the right answer. Calling out instance-level load as the missing axis of LLM routers, and quality as the missing axis of replica-pool schedulers, is well argued in §1–§2.
2. **Architecture-controlled comparison.** Running every baseline inside RouteBalance's own scheduler/batching path ("pipeline mode", §4) genuinely separates routing-policy effects from implementation effects. Most prior LLM-routing papers compare end-to-end stacks; this paper's separation is methodologically cleaner. Figure 2(c) (pure routing-policy gap inside iso-architecture) and Table 5 (architectural axis) is a defensible decomposition.
3. **Real heterogeneous cluster.** 13 vLLM instances across A100/V100/A30 with tensor parallelism on 72B and 14B is a real testbed, not a simulator. Provides direct evidence rather than emulated workloads.
4. **In-process predictor budget is a non-trivial engineering contribution.** ~3 ms per batch over $|R_B|\!\times\!|I|$ candidates with CUDA hidden from the scheduler is genuinely hard; the analytic E2E formula avoiding a separate trained head is a sensible simplification (§4 TPOT predictor paragraph).
5. **Honest limitations + open-source release.** §7 names three concrete limitations (single judge, new tuning knobs, KNN cold-start). The anonymous code release (URL in abstract) supports reproducibility, even if scope of the release is not fully described.
6. **Batching ablation is rigorous.** §5.5 + Figure 4 cover LPT-off (≤0.5% E2E), adaptive-off (5.1–6.9% E2E), and the bs=1 cliff (45.4 s E2E at λ=16, 74.6 s at λ=24). The bs=1 collapse story specifically grounds the design rationale.

## Weaknesses

1. **Quality-axis effect sizes are small and statistical significance is not established.** Table 3 shows RouteBalance's peak DQ as 0.5181 vs BEST-Route 0.5119 (+0.006 absolute) and DQ at max-throughput as 0.5079 vs BEST-Route 0.5082 (i.e., RouteBalance is 0.0003 *worse* on this column). On a 0–1 quality scale where the paper itself reports the absolute gap between adjacent model tiers (14B↔3B) as 0.015, a 0.006 gain at peak quality is well within plausible Poisson-cell variance. The paper provides no replicates, no bootstrap CIs, no confidence intervals on Figure 2 panels. A reviewer cannot tell whether RouteBalance's quality dominance is statistically significant or noise. The strongest claim that survives variance is "matches baselines' quality at lower latency and lower cost" (Table 3 captures this on cost/latency axes elsewhere); the paper should foreground that and downgrade peak-DQ rhetoric.
2. **The "upper-bound region of the Pareto" claim is weaker than the abstract suggests.** Figure 2(c) shows RouteBalance's hull at the highest throughputs (28.85 req/s) where BEST-Route has collapsed (max 18.73 req/s), but BEST-Route's *collapse* is from the per-request DeBERTa scoring saturation (Table 5 shows 33.1 s scheduling overhead at λ=30) — not from a routing-policy weakness. So the rightmost portion of the Pareto frontier where "RouteBalance dominates BEST-Route" is really "RouteBalance batches predictors and BEST-Route doesn't." This is an architectural difference the paper acknowledges in §5.3 second observation, but the same insight undermines the broader Pareto claim: the gap is partly a batching benefit, not a fused-routing benefit. Reviewers will read §5.3 + §5.5 together and note that batching alone (run any router with adaptive batching) might recover much of the headline.
3. **Single-judge dependence is more central than acknowledged.** The KNN over 14,919 prompts has its labels frozen to DeepEval-G-Eval-Llama3.1-8B. Quality numbers (peak DQ, Pareto frontier, max-q×t operating-point selection in Figure 3, RouteBalance routing decisions in production) all flow from this one judge. The "limitations" sentence is one phrase; the issue is paper-shaping. The same KNN with a different labelled set (e.g., LMSYS arena Elo, MT-Bench, ProtectAI safety scores) might rank candidates very differently, potentially changing which axis RouteBalance wins on. A judge-sensitivity ablation (rerun the labels with a second judge on a subset, show RouteBalance still beats baselines) would substantially strengthen the paper.
4. **Missing comparisons to SOTA heterogeneous serving systems.** The paper compares to model-routing baselines (AvengersPro, BEST-Route) and dispatcher-only baselines (RR/SQ/random). It does not compare to heterogeneous serving systems that *do* observe load (e.g., Llumnix is cited but not benchmarked, ALPAServe is uncited and benchmarks heterogeneous placement with statistical multiplexing). The natural counterfactual — "could a strong model router (any of the cited 10) plus a load-aware dispatcher (Llumnix or vLLM's built-in shortest-queue) match RouteBalance?" — is not directly answered. AvengersPro+SQ and BEST-Route+SQ approximate this but the dispatcher is trivial (a sorted heap on queue depth).
5. **Cost axis uses MaaS prices, not hardware TCO.** Table 1 takes per-token prices from Novita AI / Together AI public pricing pages. These prices reflect those vendors' utilisation, margin, and routing policies — not RouteBalance's own deployment economics. An operator running on owned hardware would compute cost as GPU-hour TCO divided by realised throughput, which would make 14B-on-V100 and 7B-on-A30 cost-relate very differently from the paper's prices. The cost-axis claims should explicitly scope to "vendor-priced API serving" and discuss what changes under TCO accounting.
6. **Eq. 1 normalisation choice is ad hoc and unablated.** §3.1 normalises cost and latency by their batch-wide max (`1 − x/max`), with raw quality (no normalisation), and the conclusion of §3 cites the magnitude-preservation rationale for not using min–max ("3B vs 72B 6× cost ratio survives"). This is a defensible engineering choice, but it makes scores sensitive to a single very-high-cost or very-high-latency outlier candidate — a saturated 72B/A100 with predicted E2E of 50 s would compress all other instances' lat-scores into ~1.0 and effectively turn off the latency axis. The paper does not ablate alternative normalisations (z-score, log, min–max, percentile-clip) on a held-out workload.
7. **The radar (Figure 3) operating-point selection is an editorial choice masquerading as objective.** Each panel picks the operating point that maximises *quality × throughput*. RouteBalance's polygon "containing" the baselines is in part a consequence of this objective — different objectives (cost-throughput, latency-quality, harmonic mean) might pick different operating points and produce different polygon orderings. Either the paper should ablate the objective or restrict the radar visualization to "convex-hull dominance" claims that don't depend on the picker.
8. **TTFT/TPOT SLO filter is an unevaluated system feature presented as part of the system.** §3.1 (after Eq. 2) and §4 describe a residual-CDF SLO filter pattern, but §7 acknowledges it is supported but not benchmarked. The cost filter (which IS benchmarked) is a different mechanism. A reviewer reading the abstract + §3 will assume the system delivers SLO-aware filtering; reading §7 they learn it's a future-work item. The asymmetric-credit reading is uncomfortable; either drop the SLO filter description from §3 or include even a small (one-cell, λ=12) ablation.
9. **No statistical reporting framework.** No bootstrap CIs, no error bars on figures, no replicate runs, no claim of statistical significance. Most of the paper's quantitative claims rest on single-Poisson cells per (system, knob, λ). Even three replicates per cell with a half-width on Table 3 would substantially change reviewer confidence.
10. **The "one decision" claim is partially undercut by §3.1's filter.** The system *is* two decisions in sequence: (a) admission filter (cost / SLO via Eq. 2), then (b) the multi-objective score (Eq. 1). The filter is a hard reject and the score chooses among survivors. Calling this "one decision" is rhetorically strong but engineering-wise it's a constrained scoring problem. The paper should be more precise about the relationship.
11. **Pseudocode missing.** No formal algorithm is given for the LPT-based greedy assignment described in §3.2. Reviewers interested in implementing or formalising the assignment will want to see the algorithm box. (The deleted appendix had it; bringing it back as a single boxed environment would help.)
12. **Figure 1 (architecture) is high-level and does not differentiate RouteBalance's contribution.** The diagram shows generic boxes (Scheduler, Workers, Predictors) and the same diagram could describe Llumnix or AvengersPro+SQ. A figure that highlights what's *new* (the dead-reckoning state cache, the batched per-tier predictor call, the pipeline-mode toggle) would be more useful.

## Major Issues

1. **Statistical significance is unestablished for the headline quality claim.** All quality-axis comparisons rest on point estimates from single Poisson cells. Adjacent-tier quality gap on the training set is 0.015 (paper's own number); the +0.006 peak-DQ improvement over BEST-Route is below this threshold. *Suggested fix*: at λ=12 and λ=24, run 3 replicates per cell and report bootstrap 95% CIs on Table 3's DQ columns. If RouteBalance's CI overlaps BEST-Route's at peak, restate the headline as "matches baseline quality at lower latency/cost".

2. **The Pareto-frontier dominance partly conflates routing policy with batching architecture.** §5.3 already shows that BEST-Route at λ≥18 collapses because of per-request scoring; some of Figure 2(c)'s rightmost dominance is the same effect. *Suggested fix*: add a panel or table showing the Pareto in the iso-architecture (everyone in pipeline mode, everyone batched) regime only, separated from the batched-vs-unbatched comparison. The current §5.3 "Policy vs. architecture" sentence is one line that the reader may miss; a dedicated subfigure would settle this.

3. **No ablation of the scoring formula choice.** The 3-simplex weights are swept, but the *form* of the score (raw quality + max-divide cost + max-divide latency) is not ablated. *Suggested fix*: a one-paragraph table in §5 comparing alternative normalisations (min-max, z-score, log) and quality variants (raw vs softmax-temperature) on the headline λ=12 cell.

4. **Missing baseline: model router + load-aware dispatcher (e.g., a strong router + Llumnix-style migration).** The current strongest decoupled baseline is AvengersPro pw=0.8 + shortest-queue dispatcher, which is a pretty weak load-balancing layer. *Suggested fix*: add one cell of (AvengersPro + Llumnix-style replica-pool migration) at λ=12, λ=24 to show RouteBalance is not just beating "weak dispatcher" decoupled baselines.

5. **The "in-process XGBoost in ~3 ms" claim is a key marketing point but the breakdown is hidden.** Table 5 reports total scheduling overhead but not the predictor time alone. *Suggested fix*: a stacked-bar in §5.3 or a column in Table 5 separating (KNN+embed time, XGBoost time, scoring/normalisation time, dispatch time). The 3ms number in §1/abstract should be traceable.

## Minor Issues

1. Eq. 1: the maximisation is over $x_{r,i}$ but the score $S_{r,i}$ depends on batch-wide $\max_{j\in I}\hat{C}_{r,j}$. Since this max depends only on candidates for the *same* request $r$, the maximisation factorises into independent per-request argmaxes; this is implicit but worth one sentence in the prose because the LP looks coupled.
2. Table 4 (radar picks) has every BEST-Route cell as `argmax / RR`, suggesting the threshold sweep $t \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$ never wins q×t. Worth a sentence.
3. Footnote or caption clarification needed on Figure 2(c): the convex-hull line looks like a piecewise-linear function but it's drawn over discrete operating points (system × knob × dispatcher × λ), and λ is pooled. Reader may interpret this as "single-system Pareto trace at fixed λ" rather than "envelope of all (knob, λ) cells". Caption should clarify that λ is an experimental sweep, not a knob.
4. Notation: $\hat{T}^{\mathrm{tpot}}_{r,i}$ is in the table but never appears in Eq. 1 (which uses the derived $\hat{T}_{r,i}$). The table can compress to a single E2E row with a parenthetical "derived from per-tier TPOT".
5. §3.1 says "we ablate each piece of this batching design (LPT, adaptive sizing, and the choice of fixed batch size) in §5.5" but the §5.5 section number isn't introduced until §5; forward-reference is OK but the reader might benefit from a brief one-clause preview here ("ablations show LPT is noise-level, adaptive saves 5–7%, bs=1 collapses").
6. The cluster table caption says "active routing pool" but the experimental setup (§5.1) doesn't explain whether any P100 nodes were used at evaluation time or only during latency-data generation (the paper used to mention this; the current text leaves it ambiguous).
7. (Withdrawn — self-citations are allowed under NeurIPS double-blind as long as the *prose* doesn't reveal authorship. All three references to `da2025blockbalancingloadllm` in the paper are third-person, lumped with other cited works; the bib entry having real author names is normal and required.)
8. Page 1: "submitted to 40th Conference on NeurIPS 2026" — verify the conference number (40th vs 39th) before submission.
9. §6 lists "vLLM-SR routes by static category and is subsumed by AvengersPro's pw-mix" — "subsumed" is strong; "structurally similar to" might be safer language since the architectures aren't identical.
10. Radar (Figure 3) panels at λ=6 and λ=8 have heavily overlapping polygons with all four systems near the centre — visually hard to read. Consider dropping these two panels and showing only λ ∈ {10, 12, 18, 24} where the differentiation is visible.

## Questions for Authors

1. **Statistical significance.** At λ=12, what are bootstrap 95% CIs on (DQ, throughput, E2E, cost) for RouteBalance Balance vs BEST-Route t=0.5+RR vs AvengersPro pw=0.8+RR? With single-cell evaluation, are the headline differences distinguishable from Poisson variance?
2. **Judge sensitivity.** If you re-score the test set's responses with a second quality judge (e.g., GPT-4 or a different open model) and rebuild KNN labels accordingly, does RouteBalance still trace the upper-bound region of the Pareto?
3. **Llumnix-style baseline.** What does AvengersPro + a Llumnix-style instance-aware migration dispatcher look like at λ=12, 24? This is the fairest "decoupled SOTA" comparison.
4. **Scoring formula ablation.** What does Eq. 1 look like at λ=12 with min–max normalisation (the "legacy" version mentioned in the cara_serve.py code comment) instead of max-divide? How sensitive is the Pareto frontier to this choice?
5. **Cold-start quantification.** §7(iii) acknowledges KNN cold-start. Quantitatively: if the labelled set is 1,000 prompts (not 14,919), what's the routing-quality degradation vs the full-set baseline? This would help operators planning fresh deployments.
6. **3-simplex tuning in production.** Beyond the sweep, do you have any auto-tuning, sensitivity analysis, or recommended starting weights for new workloads? Without guidance, the new $w_q, w_l, w_c$ knobs are an operational burden you've added.
7. **Pipeline-mode fairness.** When AvengersPro runs inside RouteBalance's pipeline mode, does it benefit from RouteBalance's batched-predictor amortisation in a way the original AvengersPro deployment does not? In other words, is the baseline you compare to *fairer* than how baselines actually run in production?
8. **Why max-q×t for radar?** The Figure 3 picker is one of many possible objectives. Have you checked whether RouteBalance's polygon-dominance story holds under (a) max-(q + 1/c + 1/lat) and (b) convex-hull representative point?

## Scores (NeurIPS 1–10)

- **Overall Score**: **5/10 (Borderline)**.
- **Confidence**: 4/5 (high — I know the LLM-serving area well).
- **Soundness**: 3/4 (good — methodology is mostly correct; statistical reporting is the main soundness concern).
- **Presentation**: 3/4 (good — paper is concise after the recent edits, equations are clean, figures are functional but not great).
- **Contribution**: 2/4 (fair — the "fuse routing and balancing" framing is genuinely new, but the empirical effect sizes are small and the architectural delta vs strong decoupled baselines is not fully isolated).
- **Novelty**: Medium — the framing (instance-level joint scheduling) is new but the techniques (KNN over embedding, XGBoost on telemetry, batched predictor calls, LPT, dead-reckoning) are all established; the novelty is in the combination.
- **Technical Soundness**: Medium — methods are correct but the empirical claims rest on point estimates without statistical significance, and the scoring formula is unablated.
- **Significance**: Medium — useful for operators of heterogeneous LLM clusters, but the scale (13 instances, one cluster, one judge) and the 0.6% absolute quality gain limit broader impact claims.
- **Clarity**: Medium-High — clean prose after the recent edits, single-paragraph related work, tight conclusion. A few notation details and figure-readability issues.
- **Reproducibility**: Medium-High — open-source release at anonymous URL, dataset description, scripts mentioned. Hyperparameters specified in §3.1/§4. Cluster spec in Table 1. Could be stronger with explicit training-data manifest and seed reporting.

**Recommendation**: **Borderline**. The framing and empirical setup are good; the headline quality-axis claim is shaky without CIs. If the authors add (a) bootstrap CIs on Table 3 / Figure 2, (b) a judge-sensitivity check, and (c) a Llumnix-style or strong-decoupled-baseline cell, I would raise to a clear accept. Without these, it sits at the weak-accept / weak-reject boundary depending on co-reviewer enthusiasm for the systems framing.

## Additional Notes

This is a clearly engineered, well-scoped systems paper with a real cluster and an honest comparison framework. The conceptual contribution (one decision over instances, not two) is the right framing for what the LLM-serving community will need next, and the paper executes that framing with care. The reasons it's borderline rather than accept are about empirical rigour (significance, judge sensitivity, scoring-formula robustness) rather than about the idea itself. Most of the suggested fixes are within reach of one additional sweep/ablation pass before the camera-ready window.
